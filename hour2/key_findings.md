# Hour 2 — Key Findings

1. **The aggregate-level relationship is weak (R² ≈ 0.02)** — Digital_Overload and Privacy_Concern are individually non-significant predictors of Print_Trust; no single variable drives it alone

2. **Age 55–64 is the only significant predictor (β ≈ −0.47, p = 0.001)** — they trust print *less* than 18–24 year olds, directly challenging the "older = more print-loyal" assumption

3. **The combination of overload + privacy drives trust** — High Overload + High Privacy group scores 3.146 vs 2.863 for Low + Low (Δ = +0.283); this is the Privacy Multiplier effect

4. **Mediation: partial pathway only** — Digital_Overload → Digital_Fatigue (a path, p = 0.034 ✓) but Digital_Fatigue → Print_Trust is non-significant (b path, p = 0.379 ✗); the switching mechanism is more complex

5. **No significant young segment effect in regression alone** — hold judgement until clustering results (Hour 3)
